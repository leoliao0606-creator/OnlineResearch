## 0. How to Use This Document

* **Focus on the framework, not the numbers.** Numbers go out of date; a way of looking at an industry does not. Learn the five concepts in Section 1 first, then use them to read each industry
* **Use the numbers only to sense scale.** Most sizes below are 2025 to 2026 estimates from different organizations with different definitions, and they overlap (tourism figures include airlines and hotels, for example), so they do not add up to global GDP
* **Sources**: mainly public reports from the IMF, IEA, WSTS, Gartner, McKinsey, SIPRI and IATA, plus news coverage. Because of limits in this environment, I mostly saw search summaries rather than full texts

## 1. Five Concepts for Understanding Any Industry

### 1.1 Size Is Not Profit

A large industry is not necessarily a profitable one. Some 2026 comparisons:

| Industry | Revenue or size | Profit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Global airlines | About 1.17 trillion dollars in revenue | Expected net income (净利润) of 23 billion dollars in 2026, a margin of about 2 percent, **only 4.50 dollars per passenger** |
| Global banking | 6.4 trillion dollars in revenue (2025) | 1.3 trillion dollars of net income, **the most of any industry** |
| US listed semiconductor companies | | Net margin (净利润率) of about 30 percent |
| US listed software companies | | Net margin of about 25 percent |
| US listed grocery retailers | | Net margin of about 1.3 percent |

In *Zero to One*, Thiel uses exactly this comparison between airlines and Google: airlines earn far more revenue than Google but barely make money, because they are in perfect competition (完全竞争); Google earns less revenue but keeps a large share as profit, because it is close to a monopoly (垄断) in search.

**When you look at an industry, first ask "where is the profit pool (利润池)?" rather than "how big is the market?"**

### 1.2 The Value Chain (价值链): Which Link Makes the Money

Every industry is a chain. Take AI:

> Electricity → chips (design, manufacturing, memory) → data centers (数据中心) and cloud → large models → applications

In 2026, **profits along this chain are concentrated in chips**: NVIDIA's market cap (市值) is about 5.6 trillion dollars, the largest in the world; the global semiconductor market is expected to grow about 90 percent, and memory chips about 250 percent. Meanwhile, most application companies still struggle with price competition.

Within one industry, profitability can differ by tens of times between links. When you start a company, be clear about which link you stand on and whether it will be squeezed from upstream or downstream.

### 1.3 Market Structure: Concentrated or Fragmented

* **Concentrated (集中)**: a few companies hold most of the share. Chip manufacturing is essentially TSMC; smartphone operating systems are Apple and Google. New companies find it very hard to enter head on
* **Fragmented (分散)**: thousands of small firms, with nobody holding a large share. Construction, restaurants, law firms and nursing homes are examples. Efficiency is usually low and there is plenty of room for technology, but monopoly is also hard to achieve

Thiel style opportunities often appear as **a narrow entry point inside a large, fragmented industry**: in an inefficient big industry, find a niche where a technology ten times better can quickly concentrate share.

### 1.4 Who Pays and Who Uses

In many industries, the person who uses the product and the person who pays are not the same:

* **Healthcare**: the patient gets treated, but an insurer or the government usually pays
* **Education**: the student learns, but parents or the government pay
* **Enterprise software**: employees use it, but the IT department or the boss pays

When the payer (付款方) and the user differ, a product must convince both, which makes selling harder and slower. But once you are in, customers rarely replace you.

### 1.5 What Drives Change

Industries usually change because of five forces. 2026 has a vivid example of each:

| Driver | 2026 example |
| :--- | :--- |
| Technology | The AI investment supercycle: the four largest tech giants will spend about 730 billion dollars in capital expenditure (资本开支) this year |
| Policy | The US Supreme Court struck down most emergency tariffs (关税) in February; in July the US banned new models of foreign robots and connected inverters |
| Geopolitics | The Iran war closed the Strait of Hormuz (霍尔木兹海峡), and oil briefly rose above 120 dollars |
| Demographics | The number of people aged 80 and over will grow from 143 million in 2019 to 426 million in 2050 |
| Prices of materials and energy | Copper hit record highs; energy prices hit airlines, chemicals and logistics |

## 2. The Global Economy in 2026

### 2.1 The Basics

* **Global GDP is about 126 trillion dollars** (2026, nominal, IMF April forecast)
* **The US is 32.4 trillion dollars, or 25.6 percent; China is 20.8 trillion dollars, or 16.5 percent**, together more than 40 percent. By purchasing power parity (购买力平价), China is already larger than the US
* **Growth**: the IMF's July forecast is 3.0 percent for 2026 and 3.4 percent for 2027. The IMF's view: the war shock is weighing on energy importers, while AI demand is lifting countries built into the global technology supply chain
* **The largest store of wealth is real estate**: about 393 trillion dollars worldwide (early 2025), a little over three times global GDP and more than global stocks, bonds and gold combined

### 2.2 The Most Valuable Companies (Early October 2026)

Market caps change every day, and different data sources use different dates. The top 10 below come from a single snapshot (early October 2026), so they are comparable with each other; use the numbers only to sense scale.

| 2 | Apple | 4.87 | Consumer electronics |
| 3 | Alphabet (Google) | 4.16 | Search, cloud, AI |
| 4 | Microsoft | 3.84 | Software, cloud |

**Beyond the top 10**: Tesla, at about 1.5 trillion dollars, is roughly 11th. After that comes a group of companies worth around 1 trillion dollars each, so close together that their order changes almost daily: Samsung Electronics, Berkshire Hathaway, Eli Lilly, Micron and SK Hynix.

**How to read this table**:
* 9 of the top 10 are technology companies. The one exception is Saudi Aramco, which is also the only energy company on the list
* Chips are the most concentrated theme: the top 10 includes three chip companies (NVIDIA, TSMC, Broadcom), and the group around 1 trillion dollars adds three memory chip makers (Samsung, Micron, SK Hynix). Capital markets believe the biggest value of the AI era currently sits with those "selling picks and shovels (卖铲子)"
* SpaceX is new this year: it listed on Nasdaq on June 12, 2026, raising about 75 billion dollars in the largest initial public offering (IPO, 首次公开募股) in history. A company can now grow to a trillion dollar scale before it ever goes public
* The drug company closest to the top 10 is Eli Lilly, because of its GLP 1 weight loss drugs (减重药)

### 2.3 Seven Events Every Industry Felt in 2026

1. **The AI investment supercycle**: global IT spending reached 6.37 trillion dollars, up 14.2 percent; spending on data center systems grew about 62 percent; the four largest tech giants are spending about 730 billion dollars in capex, roughly 60 percent of it on chips and servers and the remaining 40 percent or so on buildings, power, cooling and networking. The bottleneck is shifting from chips to electricity
2. **The Iran war and the closure of the Strait of Hormuz**: fighting that began February 28 closed the strait; Brent crude rose from about 72 dollars before the war to nearly 120 dollars in March, and briefly touched about 126 dollars on April 30, a four year high; container shipping rates doubled; forecasts for airline profits were cut in half
3. **Trade rules rewritten**: on February 20 the US Supreme Court ruled that the president had no authority to impose tariffs under IEEPA. The average US tariff fell from 15.3 percent to 8.3 percent, then was first replaced by a temporary 10 percent global tariff under Section 122. When that expired on July 24, it was replaced by Section 301 tariffs of 10 or 12.5 percent on 60 economies. In October the WTO raised its forecast for 2026 global goods trade growth from 1.9 percent (in March) to 3.9 percent, as AI related demand offset the shock of the war
4. **US China tech decoupling (中美技术脱钩) speeds up**: in July the US banned new models of foreign humanoid robots, quadruped robots and connected inverters; foreign drones had already been restricted
5. **The energy transition continues**: global energy investment is 3.4 trillion dollars, with 2.2 trillion for clean energy and 1.2 trillion for fossil fuels; copper prices hit records
6. **Record military spending (军费)**: 2.89 trillion dollars in 2025, the eleventh straight year of growth, with Europe up 14 percent
7. **GLP 1 drugs change consumption**: the market for obesity drugs is expected to reach 92 billion dollars in 2026, and it is starting to affect food, restaurants and retail

### A. Digital and Technology

**1. Semiconductors**
* **How money flows**: chip designers (NVIDIA, AMD, Broadcom) send designs to foundries (TSMC) for manufacturing; memory chips come from Samsung, SK hynix and Micron; the chips are sold to cloud providers, phone makers and carmakers
* **Who makes money**: both design and manufacturing are very profitable; US listed semiconductor companies have net margins of about 30 percent
* **What changed in 2026**: WSTS expects the global semiconductor market to grow about 90 percent to over 1.5 trillion dollars, possibly near 1.65 trillion based on second quarter data; memory chips are expected to grow about 250 percent to over 800 billion dollars. China is accelerating domestic substitution, and Huawei's Ascend chip output is rising fast
* **For founders**: making chips needs enormous capital, but chip design tools, verification (see Direction 2) and applications of edge AI chips are open

**2. Software**
* **How money flows**: companies pay subscriptions by number of users or by usage
* **Who makes money**: large platforms (Microsoft, Oracle, Salesforce and others) and leaders in vertical industries, with net margins of about 25 percent
* **What changed in 2026**: Gartner expects software spending of 1.47 trillion dollars, up about 15.5 percent. The biggest change is that AI agents make "per seat pricing" meaningless: if one agent does the work of ten people, a software company cannot keep charging for ten accounts, so it shifts to charging by outcome
* **For founders**: this is the most crowded area for AI startups, but vertical AI software and agent infrastructure still have many gaps

**3. IT Services**
* **How money flows**: companies pay firms like Accenture and Infosys by the person day to implement systems and maintain software
* **What changed in 2026**: spending is about 1.87 trillion dollars, 1.3 times the software market. "AI native service companies" that deliver with AI and charge by outcome are disrupting this labor heavy industry
* **For founders**: see Direction 9 in the two kinds of opportunities document

**4. Telecom**
* **How money flows**: users pay monthly for calls and data; carriers invest huge sums in base stations and fiber
* **What changed in 2026**: growth has stalled; global wireless carriers' revenue fell about 1.4 percent a year over the past five years. Starlink's direct to phone service already has 16 million users and is starting to compete with traditional networks in remote areas
* **For founders**: carriers themselves are hard to enter, but satellite communication creates new use cases

**5. Advertising, Media and Games**
* **How money flows**: brands pay for users' attention, and platforms (Google, Meta, ByteDance, Amazon) take most of it
* **Size**: global advertising revenue is about 1.3 trillion dollars in 2026, roughly 70 percent digital; games are 213.9 billion dollars, 57 percent of it mobile; GTA 6 launches in November
* **What changed in 2026**: AI assistants are becoming the new entry point for search, and "getting AI to recommend you" (GEO) is a new marketing problem
* **For founders**: creative tools, GEO, AI native games

### B. The Physical Economy

**6. Energy**
* **How money flows**: oil and gas companies extract and sell fuel; utilities (电力公司) generate, transmit and sell electricity; renewable developers build power plants
* **What changed in 2026**: global energy investment is 3.4 trillion dollars, with clean energy and fossil fuels at roughly 2 to 1. Oil investment is falling for the third straight year, while gas investment is at a ten year high. The Iran war made oil prices swing wildly. For AI, **electricity is now a tighter bottleneck than chips**: US tech giants have signed more than 9.8 GW of nuclear power deals
* **For founders**: grid (电网) software, energy storage (储能), virtual power plants (虚拟电厂), and the gap in US made inverters (see personal_fit.md)

**7. Mining and Materials**
* **How money flows**: miners extract ore, smelters process it, manufacturers buy it
* **What changed in 2026**: AI data centers and electrification are pushing up copper demand; copper briefly hit 14,527 dollars a ton in January and set new records in May; several institutions expect a copper supply gap of 150,000 to 600,000 tons this year. New mines often take more than ten years from discovery to production
* **For founders**: AI driven mineral exploration, battery recycling, supply chain (供应链) tracking for critical minerals

**8. Chemicals**
* **How money flows**: turn oil and gas into basic materials such as plastics, fertilizers and coatings, sold to almost every manufacturer
* **What changed in 2026**: China now accounts for 46 percent of global chemical sales, and Europe has fallen to 13 percent; Europe has closed about 9 percent of its capacity since 2022, and BASF shut more than 20 production units this year. Industry utilization is below 75 percent
* **For founders**: basic chemicals are a capital heavy, crowded market, but using AI to discover new specialty chemicals may be an opportunity (see Direction 3)

**9. Autos and Mobility**
* **How money flows**: carmakers design and assemble; upstream suppliers provide batteries, chips and parts; downstream are dealers, insurance and repair
* **What changed in 2026**: global EV sales are expected to exceed 23 million, about 27 percent of new cars; China sold about 63 percent of the world's EVs, and BYD targets 5 to 5.5 million vehicles this year. Robotaxis are scaling in the US and China
* **For founders**: automotive software, automotive grade verification, aftermarket and insurance data

**10. Construction and Real Estate**
* **How money flows**: developers provide the money, general contractors organize the work, and many subcontractors do it; real estate value lives mainly in the assets
* **Size**: construction is about 13 to 17 trillion dollars a year, one of the largest industries in the world; real estate is worth about 393 trillion dollars
* **Characteristics**: thin profits (US listed engineering and construction firms have net margins of about 6 percent), extreme fragmentation, and almost no productivity growth for decades. The hottest segment in 2026 is data center construction; about 40 percent of the four tech giants' capex goes to buildings, power, cooling and networking
* **For founders**: permitting, design, site inspection, construction robots. This is the classic "large fragmented industry," good for finding a narrow entry point

**11. Logistics and Trade**
* **How money flows**: ocean shipping, air freight, trucking, warehousing, last mile delivery, plus customs brokerage and trade finance
* **What changed in 2026**: the Hormuz closure affected about 10.7 percent of container capacity, and rates on major routes doubled; container traffic through the Suez Canal is still about 60 percent below 2023. Global ecommerce is about 6.9 trillion dollars, 21 percent of retail
* **For founders**: customs and compliance (合规) automation, freight software, drone delivery

**12. Food and Agriculture**
* **How money flows**: farmers grow; food passes through processing, wholesale and retail to consumers. The closer to the consumer, the thinner the margin; grocery retail net margins are only about 1.3 percent
* **What changed in 2026**: the global food import bill hit a record 2.22 trillion dollars in 2025; the war pushed up fertilizer prices; about one in eight Americans uses a GLP 1 drug, and snack spending fell about 10 percent
* **For founders**: agricultural robots, precision farming, food designed for people on GLP 1 drugs

**13. Defense**
* **How money flows**: government procurement, mostly from a few large defense contractors
* **What changed in 2026**: global military spending hit a record 2.89 trillion dollars in 2025; the US spent 954 billion dollars and China about 336 billion; Europe rose 14 percent. Drones and autonomous systems are the focus
* **For founders**: the US has new defense tech companies such as Anduril, but this field involves ethics, export controls (出口管制) and citizenship requirements; think carefully before entering

### C. Services for People

**14. Healthcare**
* **How money flows**: one of the most complex industries. Patients, doctors, hospitals, insurers, drug makers, device makers and governments all pay each other, and the user and the payer are often different
* **Size**: global health spending is about 10 trillion dollars; the US alone spends more than 5.7 trillion dollars in 2026, about 18 percent of GDP; global drug spending is expected to exceed 2.6 trillion dollars by 2030
* **What changed in 2026**: weight loss drugs took off; 29 percent of US doctors use AI to write notes from voice; in January the FDA loosened oversight of clinical decision support (临床决策支持) software; in March China approved the world's first implanted (semi invasive) brain computer interface (脑机接口) for the market
* **For founders**: automating administrative work (a huge amount of money goes to paperwork and claims (理赔)), medical devices, remote monitoring. Regulation is strict and entry is slow, but the moat (护城河) is deep

**15. Finance**
* **How money flows**: banks earn the interest rate spread (利差) and fees; payment companies take a cut of each transaction; insurers collect premiums (保费), pay claims, and invest the float (浮存金)
* **Size**: banking earned 6.4 trillion dollars of revenue and 1.3 trillion dollars of net income in 2025, the largest profit pool in the world; global insurance premiums are about 7 trillion dollars (Bain estimates about 7.1 trillion dollars for 2025)
* **What changed in 2026**: AI agents began paying on behalf of users, and payment protocols are competing; from January, US insurers excluded losses caused by generative AI from standard commercial insurance (商业保险), creating a market for specialized AI insurance
* **For founders**: agent payments, AI risk insurance, finance for small businesses. Heavily regulated, but the profit pool is large

**16. Education**
* **How money flows**: mostly paid by governments and families; schools and training providers deliver
* **Size**: about 7.3 trillion dollars, of which education technology is only 5.2 percent
* **What changed in 2026**: the evidence on AI tutoring is mixed. A randomized trial with 2,379 college students found that access to an AI tutor actually lowered grades; structured math tutoring in K12 showed small gains
* **For founders**: the payer and the user differ and adoption is slow, so this is a famously hard market. Tools for teachers and job skills training are easier entry points

**17. Professional Services (Legal, Accounting, Consulting)**
* **How money flows**: billing by the hour; clients pay for experts' time
* **Size**: global legal services are about 1.1 trillion dollars; US accounting services alone are 145 billion dollars with 1.4 million practitioners
* **What changed in 2026**: AI has greatly shortened research, review and drafting, and the billable hour is starting to wobble. 69 percent of tax professionals expect to move to fixed fees or value based pricing
* **For founders**: AI native law and accounting services, professional services priced by outcome (see Direction 9)

**18. Travel, Aviation and Elder Care**
* **Tourism**: including indirect effects, about 12 trillion dollars in 2026, nearly 10 percent of global GDP, supporting 376 million jobs
* **Aviation**: about 1.17 trillion dollars in revenue, but because of the war and oil prices, the 2026 profit forecast was cut from 41 billion to 23 billion dollars, only 4.50 dollars per passenger. A textbook case of perfect competition
* **Elder care**: the global elder care market is about 2 trillion dollars. People aged 80 and over will reach 426 million by 2050; caregiver shortages are global and especially severe in China, Japan and Europe
* **For founders**: elder care is a classic case of "certain demand, short supply, little technology." Care coordination, remote monitoring and care robots are all directions

## 4. Putting the Industries Together

### 4.1 Grouped by Profitability and by How Fast AI Is Changing Them

| | AI is changing it fast | AI is changing it slowly |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **High profit** | Software, semiconductors, finance, pharmaceuticals | Defense, telecom |
| **Low profit** | Professional services, IT services, logistics, ad agencies | Construction, agriculture, elder care, aviation, chemicals |

* **Top left**: lots of money, fast change, the fiercest competition; this is where the giants are
* **Bottom left**: low profits but being changed by AI; good for companies that "use AI to cut costs to a tenth"
* **Bottom right**: low profits, slow change, very fragmented. It looks the hardest, but it is also where technology has penetrated least. A solution ten times better could reach monopoly in a niche

### 4.2 Heavily Regulated Industries

Healthcare, finance, education, energy, defense and aviation are all heavily regulated. The upside: once you hold the licenses, latecomers struggle to catch up, which is a natural moat. The downside: entry is slow and expensive, and when policy changes, the business changes with it.

### 4.3 Threads Across Industries

After reading these 18 industries, a few threads keep appearing and are worth remembering:
1. **Electricity** constrains AI, electric vehicles and the return of manufacturing at the same time; it is one of the most important basic resources this year
2. **Population aging** affects healthcare, elder care, labor, consumption and insurance at once
3. **US China decoupling (中美脱钩)** affects semiconductors, robots, energy equipment, autos and trade
4. **Industries that bill by the hour or by the seat** (IT services, professional services, parts of software) are being pushed by AI toward outcome based pricing
5. **Large fragmented industries** (construction, elder care, agriculture, logistics) have the least technology and may hide the next generation of big companies

## 5. How to Keep Learning

### 5.1 The Rest of This Series

Each piece takes one industry apart:
1. The value chain: who sits at each link, from raw materials to the end user
2. How money flows: who pays whom, and where profits stay
3. Major players: the leaders globally, in the US and in China
4. Major turning points in history, with the winners and losers of each
5. The technology and policy changes happening now
6. The problems frontline workers complain about most
7. Opportunities of both types

**Deep dives completed**: [Energy and Power](industries/energy_power.md) · [Healthcare](industries/healthcare.md) · [Finance](industries/finance.md) · [The AI Industry](industries/ai.md)

**The five originally suggested** (construction and semiconductors not yet written):
1. **Energy and power**: AI's bottleneck and closest to your ECE background
2. **Healthcare**: the largest and most complex; once you understand it, other industries look simple
3. **Finance**: the largest profit pool in the world and where agent payments will hit
4. **Construction**: the largest fragmented industry, with the least technology
5. **Semiconductors**: the center of the AI era

### 5.2 Habits for Learning Industries on Your Own

* **Read the annual report of the industry leader**: pick the largest company in each industry and read the "business overview" and "risk factors" sections of its annual report. This is one of the fastest and most reliable ways to learn an industry
* **Listen to podcasts**: each episode of Acquired tells the full history of one big company, which builds industry intuition; Odd Lots often brings on practitioners to explain supply chains and business details
* **Read industry writing**: Stratechery on tech business models (商业模式); Construction Physics on building and infrastructure; SemiAnalysis on semiconductors; Matt Levine's Money Stuff on finance
* **Talk to practitioners**: ask three questions. What takes most of your time each day? What gives you the biggest headache? Who makes the most money in your industry, and why?
* **Pair it with your "missing log"**: as a heavy AI user, put the gaps you notice when using AI next to the industry pain points you learn about
