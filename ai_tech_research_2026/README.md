## 怎么用这个文件夹学双语 · How to Learn in Both Languages Here

这个文件夹有三层双语材料，从轻到重：

| 层次 | 在哪里 | 怎么用 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. 术语表 | [glossary.md](glossary.md) | 约 280 个核心概念，每个词有中文、英文、一句话解释、英文例句。先读"创业与战略"和"商业与财务"两节 |
| 2. 中文文档 + 英文术语 | 本文件夹里的中文文档 | 重要概念第一次出现时，括号里写英文，比如"净息差（net interest margin, NIM）" |
| 3. 英文文档 + 中文术语 | [en/](en/) 文件夹 | 完整英文版，重要概念第一次出现时，括号里写中文，比如 "net interest margin (NIM, 净息差)" |

建议的读法：先读一篇中文，记下括号里的英文词；再读同一篇的英文版，看这些词在英文句子里怎么用；最后回到术语表看例句。

The folder has three layers of bilingual material: the [glossary](glossary.md) (about 280 core terms with Chinese, English, a short explanation and an English example sentence), the Chinese documents (English terms in parentheses at first mention), and the full English mirror in [en/](en/) (Chinese terms in parentheses at first mention). A good routine: read a document in Chinese, then the same document in English, then check the example sentences in the glossary.

## 中文说明

### 任务目标

调查最近半年（2026年4月到10月初）在 AI 以及其他前沿领域出现的新技术和新研究，判断哪些可以用在产品上；之后按你的要求，扩展成两类机会的框架和世界产业的学习材料。

### 一句话结论

* **第一版**按"小团队现在能不能做"打分，选出的方向（AI 语音前台、GEO 监测、MCP 连接器）都属于风口类，适合赚快钱，但竞争会越来越激烈
* **第二版**按 Thiel 的标准（避免竞争，从小市场做到垄断）重新梳理，给出 10 个可能长成垄断的方向，例如物理世界数据飞轮、可验证的 AI、拥有发现本身的 AI 科研公司、Agent 经济的信任层。详见 [two_kinds_of_opportunities.md](two_kinds_of_opportunities.md)
* **第三版**结合你的 ECE 背景重新排序，并用四篇产业深挖帮你看懂能源、医疗、金融和 AI 这四个产业的钱怎么流、谁在赚钱、哪里有痛点

### 第一版的默认前提

第一版开始时没有指定方向，所以用的是以下默认前提；后来你补充了背景，[personal_fit.md](personal_fit.md) 按你的实际情况重新排了序：

1. 产品方向不设限
2. 团队 3 到 10 人，主要调用 API 和开源模型，不自研硬件
3. 国内和海外市场都考虑

### 调查方法

1. **广泛扫描**：学术（arXiv、顶会）、产业（各大公司和实验室的发布）、开源社区、市场信号（融资、a16z 榜单、Product Hunt），以及监管动态
2. **核实和筛选**：每项技术尽量找到一手来源并标注日期，区分"已经能用"和"只有演示或宣传"
3. **统一打分**：成熟度、可获得性、成本、产品化难度、窗口期、风险，每项 1 到 5 分
4. **对应到产品**：拿高分技术去推导产品方向

### 限制说明

* 本次运行环境的网络策略只允许搜索，大部分网页无法直接打开，所以很多数字来自搜索摘要和二手报道
* 国内竞品覆盖不全，文中标注了"未系统核实"
* 评分基于公开信息，不能替代真实的用户调研和技术验证
* 英文版是中文版的翻译，两者内容一致；如果发现不一致，以中文版为准

## English Guide

### Goal

Survey the new technologies and research that appeared in AI and other frontier fields over the last six months (April to early October 2026) and judge which could become products. At your request, this grew into a framework for two kinds of opportunities and a set of materials for learning the world's industries.

### Conclusions in Brief

* **First round**: scored technologies on "can a small team build with this now." The winners (AI voice receptionists, GEO monitoring, MCP connectors) are all hot trends (风口): good for quick money, but competition will keep intensifying
* **Second round**: reorganized everything by Thiel's standard (avoid competition, grow from a small market into a monopoly) and proposed 10 directions that could become monopolies, such as the physical world data flywheel, verifiable AI, AI science companies that own their discoveries, and the trust layer for the agent economy. See [Two Kinds of Opportunities](en/two_kinds_of_opportunities.md)
* **Third round**: reranked the directions for your ECE background and added four industry deep dives on energy, healthcare, finance and AI: how money flows, who earns it, and where the pain points are

### First Round Assumptions

No direction was specified at the start, so the first round used these defaults. You later shared your background, and [Personal Fit](en/personal_fit.md) reranks everything for your actual situation:

1. No restriction on product direction
2. A team of 3 to 10 people, mainly using APIs and open source models, no custom hardware
3. Both the Chinese and overseas markets

### Method

1. **Broad scan**: academia (arXiv, top conferences), industry (releases from major companies and labs), open source communities, market signals (funding, a16z lists, Product Hunt), and regulation
2. **Verify and filter**: find a primary source with a date for each technology where possible, and separate "usable today" from "demo or marketing only"
3. **Consistent scoring**: maturity, accessibility, cost, ease of productization, window of opportunity and risk, each scored 1 to 5
4. **Map to products**: derive product directions from the highest scoring technologies

### Limitations

* The network policy of the research environment allowed search only; most web pages could not be opened directly, so many figures come from search summaries and secondary reporting
* Coverage of Chinese competitors is incomplete; such places are marked "not systematically verified"
* Scores are based on public information and cannot replace real user research or technical validation
* The English documents are translations of the Chinese ones; if the two disagree, the Chinese version is authoritative
